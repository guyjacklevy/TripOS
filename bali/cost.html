Cost of Living in Bali in 2026: What You’ll Actually Spend

Prevoya · verified 2026 data · updated September 2026

Bali will cost you between $900 and $3,500 a month. That range is so wide it’s almost useless — and that’s exactly the problem with most cost-of-living guides. They hand you an average, and averages lie. A nomad in a Sanur studio eating nasi campur every day and a nomad in a Canggu pool villa ordering smoothie bowls are both “living in Bali,” and their monthly numbers are $2,000 apart. Neither of them is doing it wrong. They just made different decisions — most of them before their flight ever boarded.

The range is wide because three decisions drive almost everything: which area you pick, how you eat, and what kind of roof you sleep under. Make those three calls before you land and your budget stops being a guess.

The Three Variables That Set Your Number

Forget line-item budgeting for a second. Your Bali number is set by three levers, and the order matters.

Area is the biggest lever. Picking Sanur over Canggu saves you roughly $300 a month before you’ve changed anything else about how you live. Same island, same weather, same visa — different postcode, very different rent and very different prices at the cafe next door. Ubud sits between the two.

Food is the second lever. Eat entirely at warungs — the local family-run spots — and food costs you about $200 a month. A warung meal is 15,000–50,000 IDR, which is $1–$3 for real food: nasi goreng, mie ayam, grilled fish, a full plate of nasi campur. Mix warungs with a few western meals a week and you’re at $350. Go western-heavy — and western restaurant meals run $8–$25 — with smoothie bowls, flat whites and beach club lunches, and you’ll clear $600 without noticing. The gap between the all-warung nomad and the all-western nomad is $400 a month, eating on the same streets.

Accommodation style is the third. A private pool villa, a simple studio, and a co-living room are three different products at three different prices, and all three exist in every area. The villa is the Instagram default, but it’s a choice, not a requirement — and it’s usually the single line item with the most room to cut.

And a note on what doesn’t move your number much, because guides pad themselves with it: SIM and data is $15 a month, a scooter is $45–$60 (750K–1M IDR), and coworking, if you want it, is $110–$125 at the island’s best spaces. These are rounding errors next to rent and food. Optimize the big three; don’t sweat the small four.

Here’s the insight most guides skip: people make these three decisions after they arrive, by default, based on where their friends are. That’s backwards. The nomads who overspend in Bali aren’t careless — they just let Canggu choose them. Decide deliberately and the rest of this article gives you the numbers to decide with.

What Each Area Actually Costs

These are 2026 figures, verified this year — not recycled from a 2023 listicle.

Canggu

The center of nomad gravity, and priced like it. A one-bedroom villa with a pool runs $750–$1,100 a month — up 18% year on year, which is the single most important number in this guide if your budget came from an older post. A studio runs $500–$650.

Canggu is for the nomad who wants to be in the middle of everything: the coworking scene, the gyms, the events, the people. That’s a real thing and it’s worth paying for if you’ll use it. Dojo — still the island’s benchmark coworking space — is 1.8M IDR a month, about $110, and it’s where half your future collaborators are sitting. The honest part: you’re paying for proximity to other nomads, not for Bali. Budget $1,400–$1,800 a month all-in for a mid-level lifestyle — and add time, not just money, because the traffic between Berawa and Batu Bolong will eat 30 minutes of your day.

Ubud

Jungle instead of surf, quiet instead of scene. A one-bedroom runs $500–$700; a studio $350–$500. Ubud is for deep work and wellness routines — if your day is built around focused mornings and yoga, it’s the strongest pick on the island. Outpost, the anchor coworking space here, runs 2M IDR a month (about $125) and stays quieter than anything in Canggu. Monthly total: $1,200–$1,600.

The thing generic guides don’t tell you: Ubud’s cheapness partly comes back out of your pocket in transport. You’ll scooter 15–25 minutes for most things, and in rainy months the jungle damp is real — budget for a dehumidifier or a villa with good airflow, or your clothes will tell you about it.

Seminyak

One-bedrooms run $700–$950. Seminyak is nightlife-forward and more touristy, with solid WiFi and the island’s best restaurant density. Monthly total: $1,400–$1,900.

The honest read: Seminyak is where you pay hotel-zone prices for a nomad life. If you’re staying six weeks and going out a lot, it works. If you’re staying six months and working most days, you’re subsidizing other people’s holidays — Canggu gives you the same energy with more community, and Sanur gives you the same coast for half the rent.

Sanur — the underrated pick

Here’s where the math gets interesting. A spacious one-bedroom in Sanur runs $350–$500 a month. Not a shoebox — spacious, calm, in a neighborhood where Balinese families actually live. Monthly total: $1,000–$1,400 for the same mid-level lifestyle that costs $1,400–$1,800 in Canggu.

If you’ve never heard of Sanur as a nomad base, that’s exactly why rents are still reasonable. It has a flat beachfront promenade you can run on, honest warungs, fast fiber, and a 25-minute ride to Canggu when you want the scene. Sunrise instead of sunset, retirees and Balinese families instead of a scene — which is precisely why your rent is half of Berawa’s. It’s the best value on the island in 2026, full stop. We expect that sentence to age badly — Sanur’s discount is a window, not a law of nature. Use it while it’s open.

The Islands: Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan

Beautiful, cheap-ish, and not a work base. WiFi on the Nusa islands is unreliable — good enough for a weekend, not good enough for a Monday standup. Treat them as short-stay trips from a mainland base, not as a place to sign a monthly lease: two or three nights, the cliffs and the manta rays, then back to fiber internet. Any guide that recommends Penida as a long-term nomad base hasn’t tried to take a video call there.

The Three Budget Tiers

Three complete monthly budgets, built from real 2026 prices. Note that each tier has a base number and a real number — the base is what the line items add up to, the real number includes the buffer for the life you’ll actually live. Every Bali budget that ever blew up did it in the gap between those two: the base was fine, the visa run, the scooter repair, the birthday dinner in Uluwatu were not in the spreadsheet. Build the buffer in from day one and you’ll never have the “where did it all go” week.

Backpacker: $900–$1,200/month

  • Accommodation: studio or co-living in Sanur or Denpasar — $350
  • Food: warungs only ($1–$3 per meal) — $200
  • Transport: scooter rental — $45
  • SIM + data — $15
  • Workspace: cafes (buy the coffee, that’s your desk fee) — $0
  • Misc and experiences — $100

Base: roughly $710–$950 · Real number: $900–$1,200

This tier works, and plenty of people live it well — the catch is discipline. One western brunch habit or one impulse Nusa trip a week and you’re in the next tier without the villa to show for it.

Comfortable: $1,400–$1,800/month

  • Accommodation: one-bedroom villa in Ubud or studio in Canggu — $550
  • Food: warungs plus western 2–3 times a week — $380
  • Transport: scooter — $60
  • Coworking: Dojo in Canggu — $110
  • Gym — $40
  • SIM + data — $15
  • Misc and experiences — $150

Base: about $1,305 · Real number: $1,400–$1,800

This is the tier most working nomads actually land in, and it’s the one where area choice matters most — the same $1,600 buys a studio life in Canggu or a villa life in Sanur.

Premium: $2,500–$3,500/month

  • Accommodation: pool villa in Canggu — $900
  • Food: restaurant-heavy, beach club once a week — $700
  • Transport: scooter plus an occasional private driver — $150
  • Coworking: Outpost Ubud or a private desk — $125
  • Gym: boutique — $80
  • Weekend travel within Bali — $200

Base: about $2,155 · Real number: $2,500–$3,500

Worth knowing: this is also the comfortable-couple budget. Two people sharing the villa and the driver live extremely well on $2,500–$3,500 combined — per person, a couple in Bali at this tier pays barely more than one solo backpacker in Australia.

What Changed in 2026

Two things, and both matter.

Canggu rents are up 18% year on year. If your budget came from a 2023 or 2024 blog post, you’re underestimating your biggest line item by nearly a fifth. That $750 villa from the post you bookmarked is a $900 villa now. Update your numbers or update your area — this single stat is why Sanur and Ubud keep appearing in this guide.

Indonesia’s remote-worker visa (E33G) is settled now. Nomads get a renewable one-year stay permit (with a ~$60K annual income requirement), and a separate five-year “second home” track exists for those with roughly $140K in the bank. Until recently, long-stay nomads stacked 30-day extensions or ran visa runs to Kuala Lumpur — annoying, costly, and always slightly precarious. A real long-stay horizon changes the math on everything: signing yearly leases (dramatically cheaper than monthly), buying a scooter instead of renting one, negotiating long-term coworking rates. If Bali is your base and not your stopover, the paperwork has caught up with the lifestyle.
